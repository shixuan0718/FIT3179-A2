# Part B — Draft wording for the assignment template

## Domain
My assigned topic is **Topic 8: Fashion and Clothing**. The brief asks me to explore clothing and fashion products using retail datasets, compare features such as product categories or seasonal trends, and investigate change over time. My visualisation therefore focuses on three fashion product groups — **knitted apparel (HS 61), woven apparel (HS 62) and footwear (HS 64)** — together with monthly retail seasonality and clothing-price trends in Malaysia.

## Why and Who
The visualisation is designed for the **average Malaysian reader**. Its purpose is to show what types of fashion products enter Malaysia, which countries supply them, when fashion-related retail activity is strongest, how the product mix changes from 2015 to 2025, and how clothing prices have changed since 2010. These questions connect directly to the assigned topic’s focus on **product categories, seasonal trends and change over time**. The available datasets do not directly survey individual consumer preferences, so import-category shifts are described carefully as a **market-level product-mix signal**, not proof of personal preference.

## What
The visualisation combines real-world data from **OpenDOSM / the Department of Statistics Malaysia** and **UN Comtrade**. OpenDOSM provides monthly wholesale and retail trade data for MSIC 477 and state-level Consumer Price Index data for clothing and footwear. UN Comtrade provides Malaysia’s imports and exports of **HS 61, HS 62 and HS 64** from 2015 to 2025, including partner-country and product-category breakdowns. The data is filtered and aggregated for presentation rather than fabricated. MSIC 477 is a broad specialised-store retail category, so it is used as a seasonal retail proxy rather than a pure fashion-sales measure.

## How
The page is structured around the assigned Fashion and Clothing topic. Section 1 uses four different map idioms to show supplier specialisation, state clothing-price pressure, global import flows and major import hotspots. Section 2 compares fashion **product categories and suppliers** using a treemap, parallel-coordinates supplier profile and diverging partner trade chart. Section 3 addresses **seasonal trends and change over time** with a radial retail chart, state inflation heatmap and a 2015–2025 streamgraph of the product mix. Section 4 uses a long-term line chart to provide clothing-price context. These idioms were chosen so the reader can answer **where the products come from, what categories make up the market, when retail activity is strongest, and how the fashion market changes over time**.
