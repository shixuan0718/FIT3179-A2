# Part B — Draft wording for the assignment template

## Domain
My assigned domain is **Fashion and Clothing in Malaysia**. The visualisation explores clothing and fashion products through retail and trade datasets, focusing on three product groups — knitted apparel, woven apparel and footwear — together with seasonal retail activity and clothing price trends. The main story is about **product categories, seasonal trends and change over time**. Household expenditure appears only as background context and is not treated as fashion spending.

## Why and Who
The visualisation is designed for the **average Malaysian reader**. Its purpose is to show what types of fashion products enter Malaysia, where the main suppliers are located, when retail activity is strongest, how the product mix has changed from 2015 to 2025, and how clothing prices have moved over time. These patterns help readers understand changes in Malaysia's fashion market without requiring specialist knowledge of trade or economics. The page uses plain-language annotations and tooltips, and it clearly distinguishes market-level indicators from direct measures of individual consumer preference.

## What
The visualisation combines real-world data from **OpenDOSM / the Department of Statistics Malaysia** and **UN Comtrade**. OpenDOSM data is used for the 2024 Household Income and Expenditure Survey indicators by state as contextual information, monthly state-level Consumer Price Index data for clothing and footwear, and wholesale and retail trade data for MSIC 477. UN Comtrade data is used for Malaysia's imports and exports of **HS 61 (knitted apparel), HS 62 (woven apparel) and HS 64 (footwear)** from 2015 to 2025, including partner-country and product-category breakdowns. The data is filtered and aggregated for presentation; analytical values are not fabricated. Important limitations are shown on the page: HIES expenditure is total household expenditure rather than apparel-only spending, MSIC 477 is a broad specialised-store retail group, and the changing import mix is a market-availability signal rather than a direct survey of individual preferences.

## How
The page is structured as a four-part story. The first section uses four map idioms to provide market context and show fashion-specific geography: a state expenditure point map, a clothing-inflation tile-grid cartogram, a global import flow map and a proportional-symbol map of major import origins. The second section uses a treemap to compare 2025 product categories and suppliers, parallel coordinates to compare the product specialisation of major suppliers, and a diverging bar chart to contrast imports and exports by partner. The third section uses a radial bar chart to show seasonal retail activity, a heatmap to show state clothing-inflation patterns over time, and a streamgraph to show changes in the import product mix from 2015 to 2025. The final line chart contrasts clothing-and-footwear CPI with the all-items CPI. These idioms were chosen because maps answer **where**, composition charts answer **what products make up the market**, and temporal charts answer **when and how the market changes**.
