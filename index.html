What to take away
  1. Products: knitted apparel is the largest import category.
  2. Suppliers: China is the dominant source, but supplier mixes differ.
  3. Retail: seasonal swings are modest, with December on top.
  4. Prices: clothing got cheaper relative to the wider basket.

About this visualisation

Who

Designed for the average Malaysian reader, with plain-language labels and no statistics knowledge assumed.

What

UN Comtrade trade data, OpenDOSM retail and clothing-price series, household-income context and map boundaries.

Why

To show product mix, supplier structure, seasonality and price change in Malaysia’s fashion market.

How

Maps show geography; the treemap and bars compare categories; radial, heatmap, streamgraph and line charts show change over time.

Author

Ong Shi Xuan, Monash University Malaysia. Built for FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2, Semester 2, 2026. Updated 22 October 2026. Design sketch (PDF).

Data sources

  • Department of Statistics Malaysia (OpenDOSM), Consumer Price Index by state and division and state CPI inflation, January 2010 to August 2026 (CC BY 4.0). Accessed 9 October 2026.
  • OpenDOSM, Household Income and Expenditure Survey by state, 2024. Accessed 9 October 2026.
  • OpenDOSM, Wholesale and retail trade by 3-digit group, group 477 (retail sale of other goods in specialised stores, including clothing and footwear), 2018 to August 2026. Accessed 9 October 2026.
  • United Nations, UN Comtrade, Malaysia’s annual trade in HS chapters 61 (knitted apparel), 62 (woven apparel) and 64 (footwear), 2015 to 2025. Accessed 9 October 2026.
  • Maps: simplified Malaysia state boundary file supplied by the author (Sabah, Sarawak and Labuan placed at their true positions); world-atlas (Natural Earth, public domain).

Notes and limits

Household income is context, not clothing spending. Group 477 is a retail proxy, not a pure fashion series. Trade values show market supply, not individual preference. State price figures are unweighted averages, not an official national CPI.
