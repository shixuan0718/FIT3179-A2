Fashion feels personal, but the system behind it is economic: household purchasing power differs sharply across Malaysia, imports are concentrated in a handful of Asian partners, and clothing prices have moved in the opposite direction from the wider cost of living.

01

THE MACRO VIEW

Where can Malaysians afford to spend?

Household expenditure is not the same as fashion spending, but it sets the budget context in which clothing purchases happen. The maps below keep that distinction explicit.

Read this first: Putrajaya and Kuala Lumpur sit at the top of household expenditure, while lower-spending states face tighter discretionary budgets.

Uneven pressure: Sabah’s 2024 poverty rate is far above the lowest-rate federal territories, showing why a single “Malaysian consumer” is too simple.

Supply chain: China and Viet Nam dominate the largest import routes; the flow map encodes value in line thickness.

Important limitation: this is all manufacturing GDP, not textile-only output. It is used only as a backdrop for industrial capacity, avoiding a fabricated “textile hub” claim.

THE ECONOMICS OF FASHION

Who supplies Malaysia — and who buys from it?

2025 import composition

Concentration matters: partner-country blocks make clear that “fashion imports” are not one market; knitted apparel, woven apparel and footwear each have different supply patterns.

Hover a state to trace its whole profile. High income often travels with high expenditure, but poverty and inequality still separate state experiences.

Trade asymmetry: China sits heavily on the import side, while the United States and Singapore are more important export destinations.

03

RHYTHMS & SHIFTS

Fashion demand is seasonal — but less dramatically than the sketch assumed

The data does not support a large Hari Raya spike in the broad retail proxy. Rather than force the original story, the visualisation shows the stronger and more defensible year-end pattern.

December stands out: MSIC 477 sales average about 5.8% above each year’s mean in 2023–2025. Group 477 is a broad specialised-store proxy and includes more than fashion.

Cooling prices: the heatmap shows annual average clothing inflation by state. Red and blue deviations matter more than small month-to-month noise.

2025 step-up: import values rise across all three fashion chapters, with knitted apparel remaining the largest component.

04

THE PRICE STORY

The surprising part: clothing did not follow Malaysia’s wider inflation path

KEY TAKEAWAY

Across the 16 state and territory series, the clothing-and-footwear index averages about 90 in Aug 2026, while the all-items index averages about 134. In other words, clothing prices have fallen relative to 2010 while the broader basket became much more expensive.

How to read this visualisation

Who

Designed for the average Malaysian reader, with plain-language labels and no assumed statistical background.

What

OpenDOSM household, CPI, retail and GDP data combined with UN Comtrade HS 61, 62 and 64 trade data.

Why

To show how affordability, supply chains, retail seasonality and price change shape Malaysia’s fashion economy.

How

Maps establish geography; treemap and flows show composition; parallel coordinates compare states; radial, heatmap, streamgraph and line charts reveal temporal patterns.
