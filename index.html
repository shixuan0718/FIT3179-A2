Stitched Economy

What Malaysia’s household budgets, trade flows, shopping seasons and prices reveal about the clothes we buy.

Fashion feels personal, but the system behind it is economic. Households in different states have very different budgets, our clothes arrive from a handful of Asian suppliers, and clothing prices have moved in the opposite direction from the cost of living. Scroll down for the story in four parts. Hover over any chart for details; on the maps and the state charts, hovering over a state lights it up everywhere.

Words you will meet

Price index (CPI)
A number that tracks what a typical basket of goods costs. If it moves from 100 to 110, prices rose 10%.
Imports and exports
Imports are goods Malaysia buys from other countries. Exports are goods it sells to them.
Knitted and woven apparel
Knitted means stretchy clothes such as T-shirts, jumpers and socks. Woven means shirts, trousers and jackets.
Household income
What everyone in one home earns in a month, in ringgit (RM). Trade values are in US dollars (US$).
01

The macro view

Where can Malaysians afford to spend?

What clothes cost, what households earn, where clothes come from and where Malaysia makes things all differ across the map. Household spending is not the same as fashion spending, but it sets the budget in which clothing purchases happen.

02

The economics of fashion

Who supplies Malaysia, and who buys from it?

Behind every price tag is a trade deal. Next we compare what Malaysia buys with what it sells, and see how income, spending and poverty line up across the country.

03

Rhythms and shifts

Seasons, prices and imports over time

Do we shop more around festivals, and how have prices and imports moved over the years? The answer is more gradual than you might expect: seasonal swings are gentle, and the long-term trends tell the bigger story.

04

The price story

Is fashion getting more affordable?

Put it all together and one fact stands out: while the cost of living rose, the price of clothing fell.

What to take away
  1. Prices: no state faces fast-rising clothing prices. The biggest rise in the past year was +1.6% in Selangor.
  2. Trade: Malaysia buys more clothing than it sells (US$3.3 billion in, US$2.3 billion out in 2025), and China is the main supplier.
  3. Seasons: shopping peaks are gentle, with December on top, so cheap clothes are a year-round story rather than a festive one.
  4. Fairness: a household in Kuala Lumpur earns about 2.7 times what one in Kelantan does, so the same cheap shirt does not go equally far.

About this visualisation

Who

Designed for the average Malaysian reader, with plain-language labels and no statistics knowledge assumed.

What

Tables of numbers by state, month, year and trading partner from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (OpenDOSM) and UN Comtrade, plus map boundaries.

Why

To present and compare how affordability, supply chains, shopping seasons and price changes shape Malaysia’s fashion economy, and to show the trends and outliers.

How

Maps show where; the treemap and bars compare parts of a whole; the heatmap, radial bar, streamgraph and line chart show change over time. Colour and size encode values; hover and highlighting add detail.

Author

Ong Shi Xuan, Monash University Malaysia. Built for FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2, Semester 2, 2026. Published and last updated 21 October 2026. Design sketch (PDF).

Notes and limits

Household spending is total spending, not spending on clothes. Group 477 shops also sell goods other than clothing. Manufacturing output covers all manufacturing, not only textiles. State price figures are simple averages of the 16 states and territories, not an official national index. Seasonal figures use 2023 to 2025 with the yearly growth trend removed. 2025 trade values are as reported to UN Comtrade and may be revised.
